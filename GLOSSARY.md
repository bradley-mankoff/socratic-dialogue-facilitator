# Glossary — socratic-dialogue-facilitator

Project words. They mean what the v1 spec (docs/plan/2026-09-13-spec-v3.md, Epic description) says. Factory words (Idea, Spec, Epic, Bead, Workflow, Run, Human gate, Decision) mean what the Factory glossary says.

- **Target (X)**: the concept under definition (fixture: Justice, Republic Book I). One board per Symposium.
- **Symposium**: the user-facing name for one dialogue on one word (Human, 2026-10-19; replaces "board" and "room" on screen, and the room label is gone). Every Start makes a new Symposium; the earlier rule of one open Symposium per word and group was dropped once emails stopped being entered by the host (Human, 2026-10-24).
- **Board**: the code's name for a Symposium's state (the board-state machine and its store). Never shown on screen.
- **Host**: the participant who starts a Symposium, like the host of a party game. On one start screen the host enters their own name and email, the word, the names switch, the play mode and what the AI player may do, then adds people with invitation links. The host may switch the play mode at any time and decides whether names appear in the closing diagram; everything about the argument is a vote. Every other participant is invited by the host.
- **Participant** (also **player**): a person in a Symposium. The invitation link is their identity. They name themselves (names on) or get a random animal (names off), and enter their own email for turn and vote emails; nobody sees another participant's email, the host included (Human, 2026-10-24).
- **Invitation link**: a personal link the host makes with "Add a person" and sends from their own phone through its share menu (Messages, Mail, WhatsApp and so on), with an optional private note only the host sees, such as "for Sam" (Human, 2026-10-24). It carries no protection: whoever opens it is that participant, because nothing in a Symposium is private. A first visit opens the welcome page, which asks for name (names on), email and color (names on), then lets them in.
- **Lobby**: the small "who is here" panel on a Symposium page, listing each participant (by name or animal) and whether they have joined yet, never emails. Display only.
- **Private message**: a message from one participant to one other, kept inside the forum and shown only to those two, without revealing either email.
- **Source note**: the small parenthetical after a dictionary definition, like "(Wiktionary)", linking to the entry. License and access date stay recorded but are not shown.
- **Active definition**: the single definition under elenchus, front-center; counterexamples attach to it.
- **Defending order**: a random order of the Symposium's members (the host included, and the language model when the host allows it to defend), shuffled when the host starts it and shown to everyone (Human, 2026-10-24). Only the first member proposes the first definition, picked from the dictionaries or written; that member is its **defender** and may revise it until a discard vote passes, and then the next member in the order proposes theirs. The order starts again from the top when it runs out. Members who have not opened their invitation link are skipped until they do; members invited later go to the end. It replaces first-come claiming.
- **Challenge order**: a second random order of the members (the language model when the host allows it to offer counterexamples), shuffled separately (Human, 2026-10-24). On their turn a member offers one counterexample or passes, and the turn moves on once the Symposium has voted on that counterexample, so one challenge is handled at a time. The current defender is skipped. It starts again from the top when it runs out, with the same skip and late-joiner rules as the defending order.
- **Discard vote**: a majority question that sets the active definition aside in the history and hands the next definition to the next member in the defending order (Human, 2026-10-24). The defender votes too. A counterexample the Symposium upholds never discards; it sends the defender back to revise.
- **Skip vote**: a majority question that passes the turn of a member who is away. A member may also pass their own turn. There are no time limits (Human, 2026-10-24).
- **AI player**: a language-model member of a Symposium, taking whichever turns the host allows (defending, offering counterexamples, or none) (Human, 2026-10-24). It names itself like any player and is told never to reveal that it is an AI; unless the host turns names on, it also takes each turn after a random wait of minutes to hours, so its speed does not give it away. It may ask the question agent like any player, and the question agents are never told which player is the AI. Kept apart from the question agent so the two never mix.
- **Diotima**: the name of the question agent, after the wise woman whose teaching Socrates passes on in Plato's Symposium (Human, 2026-10-24).
- **Apollodorus**: the agent that keeps the closing diagram, after the man who retells Plato's Symposium (Human, 2026-10-24). After every change, code builds and lays out the diagram, and Apollodorus looks at a picture of it, moves things to fix overlaps and crowding, and writes the captions that tell the story. It never changes what happened.
- **Names switch**: one host setting per Symposium, names on or names off (Human, 2026-10-24). Names on: players appear by name with their player color, the AI player is labeled as the AI, and votes show who voted which way. Names off: each player appears only as a random animal, its icon with its name beside it ("Otter"), no two alike, with no name and no color, the AI player looks like everyone else, and votes are anonymous. The question agents are never told which player is the AI either way. Before the closing diagram is made, the host decides whether names appear in it, whatever the switch was during play; showing names also marks which player was the AI.
- **Player color**: with names on, the color each player chooses on arrival from about twelve colors easy to tell apart on a phone (colors already taken are greyed out), used for their contributions and their chat marker; their icon is the first letter of their name in that color (Human, 2026-10-24).
- **Play mode**: a per-Symposium toggle for how it is being played at the moment (Human, 2026-10-24). Async ("by email"): turns and votes go out as emails, and replies come back into the Symposium. Live: no emails at all. The host may switch it at any time. When the AI player is hidden, it waits minutes to hours before each turn in async mode and about a minute in live mode.
- **Receipt**: after a Symposium, the host gets its model costs itemized by player (Human, 2026-10-24). The host supplies one API key, or signs in with OAuth, once on the forum's settings page, and picks the model there, starting on an extremely cheap one; every agent in every Symposium uses it. The receipt is shown to the host and emailed to them, with one line per player for their private questions to Diotima, a line for the AI player's turns, and a shared line for Apollodorus and the open chat; when a subscription is used instead of a key, the receipt gives the API-equivalent price from the tokens used.
- **Turn email**: in async play mode, the email a player gets when it is their turn: "it's your turn, pass or play", with the word, the current definition, the latest counterexample and its vote, and a link. Replying with a definition, a counterexample or "pass" takes the turn, so a Symposium can be played entirely by email (Human, 2026-10-24). Apollodorus labels the kind of an emailed counterexample, and the player can fix the label on the site.
- **Vote email**: in async play mode, the email each player gets when a vote opens; replying "yes" or "no" casts their vote (Human, 2026-10-24). Turn and vote emails are the only notifications, sent from the forum's own Gmail account, and nobody ever sees another player's email address, the host included. People enter their own emails after opening the personal link the host texted them.
- **Phone first**: pages are designed for a phone held upright, and for async play first (Human, 2026-10-24).
- **Question agent**: the language model every player can ask about the Symposium, privately or in an open chat everyone sees (Human, 2026-10-24). One open instance and one private instance per player; none of them knows which player is the AI player, so all players are treated alike. It never speaks unless asked.
- **No score**: a Symposium has no score and no winner (Human, 2026-10-24). Votes still decide counterexamples, discards, skips and closing, but the outcome is the closing diagram, fit to print and frame, and a group with a sense of shared values. There is no vote to go back to an earlier definition: a later defender may simply propose the same definition again.
- **Call limit**: each member may call at most three discard votes and at most three skip votes in one Symposium; a call whose vote passes does not count against the three (Human, 2026-10-24). The two limits are separate: using discard calls never uses up skip calls.
- **Counterexample shapes 3a / 3b**: 3a is IS-X-but-fails-the-definition; 3b fits-the-definition-but-is-NOT-X.
- **Iteration chain**: revision history as new version nodes, never edits; superseded nodes freeze with tallies intact.
- **Canvas**: closing summary that walks the debate as a story — who offered each definition, who raised each challenge, how that lands on the map. Attempts as nodes, refutations as labeled edges, active chain highlighted. PNG-only export. Its graph allows cycles: a rejected counterexample returns the room to the earlier attempt it struck.
- **Attempt**: plain user-facing word for one definition node on the canvas (Attempt 1, Attempt 2, the live one marked active). Machine ids never show on screen.
- **Set aside**: plain user-facing word for the tray box on the canvas, carrying earlier counterexamples forward with a caption saying each can be reopened on the active attempt or dropped.
- **Handle**: the code's board-local identity for one participant, bound to their invitation link. Voiceprint enrollment and provisional labels (tabled with the voice stack) also bind to handles.
- **Census**: for a question Q, BoardCensus(Q.openedAt) plus handles that subsequently cast on Q.
- **Q**: every majority question: counterexamples, discard votes, skip votes and closing (Human, 2026-10-24; mode switches, Socrates swaps, active-claim switches, tiebreak invocations and ingress selection are gone from the forum).
- **Standing**: a counterexample view — stands-ballots outnumber rebutted-ballots; 0–0 is not standing.
- **Refuted**: node-level verdict — some attached counterexample is standing AND 2 × stands > |Census| (strict majority).
- **Tray**: visible carry of unresolved counterexamples from a superseded node as carried_unvoted; press re-opens as a new Q on the active node, discard is system-logged.
- **Socrates seat**: dropped (Human, 2026-10-24). There is no questioner seat, no vote to hand it over and no start-page field for it; the host enters the word, and the defending and challenge orders decide whose turn it is.
- **Ingress**: live audio path per session — HostMic XOR GuestTabs, exactly one; switching is governance.
